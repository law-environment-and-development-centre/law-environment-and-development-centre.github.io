About Us

The Law, Environment and Development Centre (LEDC) investigates the dynamic relationships between the fields of law, environment and development.

The Law, Environment and Development Centre (LEDC), based in the College of Law, provides a focal point to expand the increasing interest in the dynamic relationships between the fields of law, environment and development. The main goal of the centre is to advance research and teaching and explore the role each of these disciplines plays in realising sustainable development and natural resource use, particularly in the Global South.

LEDC adopts multidisciplinary approaches in researching key issues in environmental law and development today, including water, land use, forests, climate change, intellectual property, rights of nature and indigenous peoples’ rights. Its research focuses in particular on topics of relevance to the South and to North-South relations.

LEDC is a member of the IUCN Academy of Environmental Law, the largest network of environmental law education providers, and co-hosts the European Research Council-funded project Addressing the Multi-scalar Dimensions of Sectoral Water Conflicts: Lessons from South Asia (WATCON).

Prospective PhD candidates are welcome to contact Professor Philippe Cullet or Dr Birsha Ohdedar about supervision.

Research themes

The Law, Environment and Development Centre undertakes research in various areas of environmental law of contemporary relevance. The centre's research focuses in particular on topics of relevance to the South and to North-South relations:

Water

Right to water, groundwater, governance and institutions; environment; regulation and privatisation

Water & sanitation

Right to water & sanitation, wastewater, manual scavenging and sewage workers; gender

Natural resources

Land; mining, plant genetic resources for food and agriculture; multinational companies

Forests

Governance, biodiversity, energy, forest users' rights and trade

Climate change

Common but differentiated responsibility; Clean Development Mechanism, emissions trading

Biosafety

Biotechnology, in particular agro-biotechnology in the South; precautionary principle; liability regimes

Biodiversity

Access and benefit sharing; equity; farmers' rights; intellectual property rights; traditional knowledge

Meet the Team

Professor Philippe Cullet

Philippe convenes the Law, Environment and Development Centre (LEDC), and co-convenes the LLM in Environmental Law and Sustainable Development, the joint SOAS-NLUD LLM/MA in Environmental Justice and the MRes in Law. He is also the Editor of the Law, Environment and Development Journal (LEAD Journal), a peer-reviewed academic journal hosted by the LEDC.

Read more about Philippe's work

Dr Birsha Ohdedar

Birsha is the deputy director of the Law, Environment and Development Centre (LEDC), and co-convenes the LLM in Environmental Law and Sustainable Development. He is a Senior Lecturer in Climate Change and Environmental Law, an Editor of the LEAD Journal, and co-coordinates the SOAS Environmental Justice Clinic.

Read more about Birsha's work

Dr Nate Palmer

Nate is the Experiential Learning and Engagement lead at the LEDC, and is a Lecturer in International Environmental Law at SOAS, convening modules including International Environmental Law and Alternatives to Sustainable Development: Rights of Nature and Harmony with Nature. Nate is also an expert member at the UN Harmony with Nature Programme, serves as an assistant editor at the LEAD Journal, and co-coordinates the SOAS Environmental Justice Clinic.

Professor Martin W Lau

Martin is Professor of South Asian Law at SOAS. His work covers the laws of South Asia, comparative environmental law and Islamic law.
